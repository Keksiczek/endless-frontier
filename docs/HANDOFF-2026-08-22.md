## 2. Combat, measured

`ZZBattleDiag` (`EF_DIAG=1 swift test --package-path Core --filter ZZBattleDiag`,
about a minute) prints steps allotted, steps run, seconds, why it ended, volley
kinds, wound parts, and what the colony is holding. **Read it before tuning any
combat number.**

### What was wrong

- **The raiders had no ranged attack at all.** `SiegeEngine.loose` filtered on
  `side == .colony`, so every fight the game has ever run was one-sided
  shooting: the colony loosed at people walking toward it, then everybody swung.
  `returnFire` is the other half; a warband's arms come from the era it fights
  in (`raiderArms`) — stones, then arrows, bolts, ball, bullet, beam.
- **The clock was the referee.** `isFinished` ended on `step >= steps`, so a
  raid could be over in **seven seconds** with both sides intact. It ends on the
  **rout** now (`Siege.routAtShare` — seventy per cent of the warband's weight
  gone and they go home).
- **Nothing but bows was ever in anybody's hands.** `armed 17 of 68 —
  ["none": 13, "arrow": 4]`, shelf empty. Three stacked faults, §2.2.

### 2.1 The clock has to stay as a backstop

Taking it out entirely was tried and **measured**: `meleePerStep` divides the
line's weight by `steps` — one full weight across the fight — so a line that can
only ever deal its own weight cannot take a warband past the rout threshold, and
the fight *cannot end*. Ninety raiders against six defenders ran eighty steps
for 90 → 56 strength with three hundred more to go. If anybody wants an
unbounded fight, every per-step number has to be re-read as a rate first
(rule 80).

### 2.2 Why nobody was armed

1. `CombatEngine.weaponProfile` rebuilt a two-field profile — damage and class —
   and dropped `projectile`, `range`, `caliber`, `shots`. So the one query that
   asks what somebody is holding could only answer "ranged" or "melee", which is
   why **eighty-two weapons were drawn as three silhouettes**.
2. `bestGear` ranks by `worth`, which is damage. A bow loses to an axe every
   time, so the quartermaster made blades and only blades. `rangedShare` keeps
   two in five of an armed line able to shoot.
3. **A standing order held its bench for ever.** `CraftingEngine.workableBenches`
   takes the oldest workable order per shop, and a standing order never
   finishes. The builders' timber order was older than every batch of spears.
   An order with an *end* takes the bench first now (rule 83).

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| armed | 17 of 68 | **49 of 69** |
| on the line | 16 | **27** |
| carrying something ranged | 4 | **17** |
| kinds in hand | `arrow` | `arrow, stone, none` |

### 2.3 Wounds

`BattleMoment` carries `part` and `wound`, so a beat is "a stab to Mara's left
arm". The anatomy was already there — `Body` has head, torso, two arms and two
legs, each with a condition, and `condition <= 0` already sets `missing`. What
was absent was *recording* it, and the wound kind now comes from the weapon
(`WoundKind.from`) rather than from a die.

### 2.4 Two faults where defending made things worse

- `shoulder` parts bodies without caring which side they are on, so a raider
  could be squeezed **through** an intact line; and `isInside` is a circle of
  `wallReach` while the watch forms up at `formUpReach`, *inside* it, so the
  back rank of a well-manned line stood in the "among the stores" circle.
  Measured: a town of sixty held its line, lost nobody, and was plundered, while
  a town of ten lost nothing.
- A broken line used to **end** the fight, so losing it protected the grain.

## 3. The council, measured

`ZZCouncilDiag` (`EF_DIAG=1`, ~20 min) prints, per decade, what the colony can
build, what is spilling, which clause answered and what it picked.

The state a screenshot caught: year 28, medieval, **600 materials of 600**, an
observatory standing and no warehouse. `buildableHere` returned **one**
definition — a well. Three faults:

1. `CraftingEngine.maxOrders` is twelve and the council took all of them, so the
   player's own orders were silently **refused**, the quartermaster never got a
   slot, and every material trickled. `councilBenchShare = 8`.
2. Those orders were walked in `Set.sorted()` order — the first letter of an
   item id was the colony's industrial policy. `shoppingList` sorts by how many
   buildings the material unblocks, which needed `wantedHere` (every guard
   except "the made things are on the shelf").
3. `canAffordToKeep` weighs a building's **materials production** against its
   upkeep, and a warehouse produces nothing — so a colony at its cap was
   refused, for ever, the building that raises the cap.

After: `able` runs 9–29 instead of 1, the store cap climbs 500 → 44 300 over 250
years, population 402. Full write-up in `docs/COUNCIL.md`.

**Not fixed, and visible in the same run:** knowledge sits at 120 against a cap
that reaches ten thousand — research income is close to zero.

## 4. What Keks asked for next, in his own order

Numbered because the next chat should work down them, not pick.

1. **Roads on the settlement map, and paths between buildings.**
   *"silnice jsou jen na mape sveta i kdyz jsem je chtel viditelne na mape osady
   … obecne stavby jsou takove sve."* The two are one feature: the settlement
   has no ways in it, so a village reads as boxes on grass. `RoadNetwork` is
   per world-hex; what the settlement wants is worn tracks between the lots
   people actually walk between — and `AgentMotion` already knows every journey
   anybody makes, so the traffic is measurable rather than invented.
2. **Building a road through the map, not through a list.** The current
   affordance is *Ways from here* in the region panel — a list of neighbours
   with prices. `docs/ROADS.md` §8 says a tap on a hex edge is the obvious one
   and that it wants the drawing first; the drawing exists now.
3. **Purposeful fighting.** *"chovat se že utoci nejak cilene ne že ted vedle
   sebe hybaju dve cary lidi a sem tam nekdo pribehne nebo zmizi."* `aim` picks
   the nearest enemy, every step, for everybody — so the whole field converges
   and nobody has an intention that lasts longer than one step.
4. **The other peoples.** *"nyni jsou to basic postavicky a stany na mape."*
   Asked and **answered**: *"idealne plne narody, ale klidne bych to nejdriv
   trochu osekal at neni tak slozita simulace — jen at vypadaji a chovaji se
   nejak jako hlavni pawni."*

   So it is staged, and the stages are worth keeping apart because the first is
   cheap and the second is a second game.

   **Stage one — they look and behave like people.** A tribe's hex draws as a
   *place*: `LocalMapGenerator` already makes ground for any region, `Settlement`
   already draws through `SettlementRenderer`, and `Pawn` already has a body, a
   look and a day. What a tribe needs is a small roster of real pawns and a few
   real buildings, walking their own ground the way colonists walk yours —
   drawn by the code that already exists, not by a second renderer. Their
   numbers stay `DiplomacyEngine`'s: `Tribe.population`, `standing`, `genes` go
   on deciding everything, and the pawns are what that number *looks like*.
   Nothing new simulates; `AgentMotion` is presentation and may not write state
   (rule: the canvas never feeds the simulation).

   **Stage two — they are a colony.** The same engines run on their settlement:
   food, housing, births, a council. That is the honest end state and it is also
   a second simulation's worth of ticks per tribe, so it wants measuring
   (`ZZStewardProbe` shape) before it is switched on for five peoples at once.

   The line between the stages is exactly this: **stage one adds nothing to the
   tick.** The moment a tribe's pawns eat, it has crossed into stage two.
5. **A dynamic assembly.** *"snem by mohl byt dynamicky ze by lide volili dle
   svych vlastnosti zkusenosti nazoru."* Today a law passes on one aggregate.
   `LawDefinition` already scores a pawn's genes (`industry`, `fertility`,
   `sociability`, `courage`) — the machinery for a per-colonist vote exists and
   nothing counts them one at a time.
6. **Bandits with somewhere to come from.** See §5 — this is a design note
   rather than a bug, and it is the one that makes raids worth having.

## 5. Bandits should come from a place

Keks: *"k banditum napis aby byly vice ruznorodi treba chodili ze zakladen na
mape co nejsou zadna frakce."*

Today `BanditEngine` conjures a warband out of nothing, sized off `temptation`
— how full the stores are — and names it from a per-era list. Nothing about the
raid exists before it arrives or after it leaves. That is why a raid is an
interruption rather than an event: **there is nobody to have a war with.**

The shape:

- **A camp is a place on the map.** A new `RegionKind` — outlaw camp — placed at
  generation like `ruins` and `lostCity` are, on ground that is far enough from
  anybody to be theirs. It has a strength, a temper and a name of its own, and
  it is **not a `Tribe`**: no standing, no diplomacy, nothing to negotiate with.
  That is the whole point of them, and it is what makes them different from the
  neighbours who *can* be talked to.
- **Raids come from a camp**, so `Siege.approachBearing` points at somewhere
  real, the road they walk in on is a road you could have cut (`RoadEngine.cut`
  already does this for tribes), and a big raid empties a camp for a while.
- **A camp can be found and cleared.** `SiteEncounter` already knows how to make
  a place out of things, and `LocalPOIEngine` already sends a party to one and
  can lose people doing it. Clearing a camp stops its raids for a season and
  leaves the loot they took — which turns "a raid happened" into "a raid
  happened, and we know where they went".
- **Variety comes from what a camp is**, not from a name list: a deserters'
  camp is well-armed and few, a starving band is many and poorly armed, a
  raiders' hold is neither and has walls. That is one field on the camp
  (`kind`), and `raiderArms` already reads an era to decide what they carry —
  it can read a camp instead.
- **Growth.** A camp that is left alone gets stronger; a camp fed by a colony
  that keeps paying it off gets stronger faster. That is the sink `docs/RULES.md`
  §16 keeps asking for: something that scales with *the world* rather than with
  a building count.

Two constraints worth writing down before anybody starts:

- **Sizing must not be off the stores alone.** It is now, and it is why a band
  of four attacks a colony of sixty-eight and is over in six seconds. A camp's
  strength is the camp's own, grown over time; the stores decide whether they
  bother coming, which is what `temptation` should have been for all along.
- **They are still not a faction.** The moment a camp has standing, a grudge and
  a marriage alliance, it is a tribe with a different hat, and the thing that
  made bandits interesting — that you cannot buy them off for ever — is gone.

EF_DIAG=1 swift test --package-path Core --filter ZZBattleDiag    # ~1 min
EF_DIAG=1 swift test --package-path Core --filter ZZCouncilDiag   # ~20 min

**Disk is still the recurring hazard.** Each `--scratch-path` is about 370 MB
and a full run needs room on top; the machine hit zero twice yesterday and Swift
fails inside the index store with `No space left on device`, which reads like a
compiler bug and is not one. Delete finished scratch paths as you go.

**One SwiftPM lock per scratch path.** Two runs on the same path queue silently.
