## 1. Research: the tree was over by year seventy

`ResearchProbe` (new, `EF_PROBE=1 swift test --package-path Core --filter
ResearchProbe`, ~25 min) settled an argument between two readings. The council
diag showed knowledge banked at 120 against a cap of ten thousand — "income is
nearly zero". Keks, playing: *"přijde mi, že zkoumají docela rychle a mají to
během pár desítek let z většiny za sebou."*

**Keks was right and the bank was a red herring**: an empty bank is what a
fast spender looks like.

```
before   37 techs, all of them done by year 70    5/3/8/6/6/6/3 per decade
after    60 techs, all of them done by year 130   3/3/2/3/5/4/6/5/6/7/6/8/2
```

**The fix was not "write more techs".** With six levers in the whole game,
twenty more studies would have been twenty more `+1 knowledgeOutput` —
research that produces research. Five new `ResearchStat`s came first, each
with a seam in an engine and a test that runs it: `huntYield`, `gatherYield`,
`carryCapacity`, `wallStrength`, `trainingSpeed`. Then 23 techs that pull
them, and costs set against the **measured** income curve (about three years
of the income of the age a study belongs to; it had been well under one).

`ResearchStat`'s documentation promised a test that fails when a stat is never
read by an engine. That test existed — inside `NewPOIKindTests.swift`, of all
places — but only covered the stats somebody had remembered to write a case
for. There is a source-walking guard beside it now.

**Still open, and it is a design question rather than a bug:**

- The tree still runs out at year 130 of a game that goes on for centuries.
  After that the only sinks are the repeatable studies, whose price escalates
  (24 000 → 83 000 over the last seventy years).
- Late income outruns any fixed price: the probe measured **16 249 knowledge
  in one year** at year 180 against 2 000-odd at year 60. A study priced in a
  constant cannot stay meaningful across that. If the tree should last the
  whole run, the lever is a cost that scales with the colony rather than more
  content.
- **Eras lag the tree**: still medieval at year 80 with 32 of 60 studies done,
  modern only at 130. Era milestones are in `eras.json` and nobody has looked
  at them against this curve.
- The probe's `in/yr` column is a *net* of the bank, so a year that finished a
  study reads negative. It is honest about pacing and useless as an income
  figure; if income itself matters, measure `knowledgeOutput` per settlement
  instead.

## 2. The map's names all rhymed

*"Názvy map jsou skoro stejné, nudné, v okolí mám to samé."* Two causes,
neither of them the word list:

- **One shape for every name** — *epithet + stem + suffix*, always.
- **Neighbours shared their parts.** The index was `hexIndex + seed`,
  `hexIndex` walks in ones, and the epithet changes once every `stems ×
  suffixes` — so "Far" sat on everything within reach.

Four shapes now, and the index is scattered by a unit modulo the space, which
keeps the bijection (no two hexes can collide) while putting neighbours in
different parts of it.

    Vnitřní Tichovka · Západní Psí haluz · Dolní Lipovka · Doubíkov ·
    Horní Tetřeví hvozd · Vlčí úbočí · Kozí stráň · Železovice · Větrov

**The Czech banks are built so the generator cannot produce bad Czech**: the
adjectives and epithets are the soft `-í` forms, which are the same word for
all three genders. The first pass was not, and produced "Malé Tichovka" and
"Nové losí sráz" — the generator has no way to know a noun's gender. Guarded
by a test.

## 3. Things the simulation had and the screen did not

Keks: *"ať je vše, co máme, nakreslené i na mapě osady jako reálné věci —
silnice jsme museli dělat napodruhé."*

An audit of `Settlement`'s fields against what the canvas draws found three
gaps. Two are closed:

- **Captives.** `CaptiveEngine` has kept whole `Pawn`s — a name, a trade, a
  `trust` that walks from *out the gate the first dark night* to *one of us* —
  and the only sign a colony had any was a journal line on the day one joined.
  `SettlementCaptives` draws them; `CaptiveCard` says who they are.
- **What is in a store.** The first pass drew *how full*; a warehouse of
  timber and one of hides were the same picture. `SettlementInterior.Goods`
  reads the colony's own `stockpile` and lays it out by kind.

Still not drawn: **craft orders** (a bench with an order looks like a bench
without one) and **inventory** (equipment nobody is carrying). Neither is as
bad as the captives were.

## 4. How to measure what is above

```bash
EF_PROBE=1 swift test --package-path Core --filter ResearchProbe   # ~27 min
EF_DIAG=1  swift test --package-path Core --filter ZZNameSample    # instant
EF_DIAG=1  swift test --package-path Core --filter ZZOutlawDiag    # ~30 min
```

**One run at a time** — two SwiftPM runs starve each other, and the perf
guards (`TribeCampTests`, `OfflineCatchUpTests`) fail on a loaded machine.
