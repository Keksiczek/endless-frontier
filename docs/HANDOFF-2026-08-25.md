## 2. This batch — the four open items Keks picked

### Wood: who gets it (was §1)

Measured on his save: `saw_timber` and `burn_charcoal` both want `wood`, the
valley's wood arrives a few sticks at a time, and the timber order was placed
at tick 60 — so the saw took every stick for a century, charcoal stayed at
zero, `smelt_iron` never ran, and the whole iron half of the item tree was
unreachable.

**A standing order yields the bench when it already has plenty *and* something
else is short of an input they share.** Contention is the trigger, not the
shelf: a flat "stop at eighteen" was tried first and was worse — bricks froze
at eighteen while clay piled to ninety and the building programme slowed,
because nothing else wanted clay and stopping bought nobody anything (rule 16).
A job with an end (`wanted`) never yields.

Sixty years from the save, best run yet: **279 souls** (258 before, 154 with
the flat cap), **modern era reached**, bricks 7 → 274, charcoal steady at 1–3,
iron ore falling 321 → 273 — smelting finally runs.

### Water outside the router (was §2)

`isWater` was asked in a handful of places and none of them was building or the
wild. Now:

- **`ColonyBuilder.drowned(in:)`** returns the deep-water tiles of the build
  grid, folded into the occupancy set — so every placement path refuses them
  for free, with no new question threaded through six functions. Deep only: the
  shallows are a beach, and a jetty belongs on them.
- **`AnimalEngine.step(…, deep:)`** stops a stride that would end out of its
  depth, backing off along the same line. A beast *already* in deep water (an
  old save, a shore that moved) is not frozen — it may move, just not further
  out.

### Herbs (was §5) — **not broken, do not fix**

The deposit reads 0.1% of capacity and the handoff called it "the same shape as
wood, unmeasured". Measured: the shelf holds **486 herb bundles**. The chain
flows; `depositFloorFactor` is doing exactly what it was written for — a
nearly-exhausted patch still yields because colonists range farther. The
contrast with wood is the whole point: there the shelf was **also** zero.

### `WalkRoutes` profiled (was §6)

`SettlementRoute`'s open set was a linear scan for the cheapest node, with a
comment claiming a small frontier made that "measurably not the cost here" —
which nobody had measured. It is **4.4 ms a leg** on a town of sixty, and a
colony re-routes every walker at once whenever its layout changes. Replaced
with a binary heap on the same tie-break (a route must not change shape because
it got faster): **1.8 ms**.

**The timing assertion was written and then taken out again**, and that is
worth knowing before someone writes it a third time. The same code measures
1.8 ms alone, 2.6 with one other suite running and 3.9 with several; a ratio
against a straight line swings 409 → 696 for the same reason, because a
straight line is a dozen integer steps and noise swamps it. What is pinned
instead is the **shape**: a route stays within 3× the Manhattan straight line,
which a search that stopped exploring sensibly would break, and which is the
same on any machine.

## 3. Open

1. **The settlement span still does not follow the grid.** `grownOutward` takes
   a ring of four whenever a building will not fit; the span does not, so a
   growing town's tiles shrink. Papered over with `Camera.opening` at 2.8. The
   real fix drags the charted ground (`LocalMapGenerator`) and the rock
   clearance (`StoneEngine.colonyClearance`) with it.
2. **Research runs out at year 130** of a 200-year game, and eras lag the tree
   — medieval at year 80 with 32 of 60 studied. Untouched since 23 August.
3. **Expeditions still walk on water.** Building and the wild now ask;
   `POIExpedition` does not. `PathEngine.waterDepth` is the one function.
4. **Outlaw raid cadence**: 7 in two centuries against 63 from peoples and 57
   from the wild.
5. **`roofEnough` was guessed, not measured** (`StewardEngine`).
6. ~~The 150 ms layout budget in `TribeCampTests`~~ — **done in this batch.**
   It cried wolf four times in a week (about 2 ms alone, 210–830 ms inside a

   comes out near 4×; the linear-search-inside-a-grid-scan it was written for
