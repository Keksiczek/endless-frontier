## What this session did

1. **A raid landed on the wrong colony.** `stageRaid` opened the siege on
   `settlements.first`; `advanceSiegeStep` and `startSiegeLoop` both worked on
   the *selected* one. With more than one settlement that is a world paused for
   a fight nothing is carrying forward — a frozen game. The loop now steps
   every besieged settlement and the screen watches `runningSiege` rather than
   the viewed one's.
2. **The fight was drawn on a stopped clock** (rule 103). `withinStep` measured
   the share of a simulation step elapsed against the *world* clock — and a
   raid pauses the world. The siege loop meanwhile resolved a step every 1.4
   real seconds, so the step counter outran a `continuousTick` pinned at
   `tick + 1` inside a dozen seconds, the subtraction clamped to zero and
   stayed. `SettlementBattle.Beat` stamps each step as it lands, in the
   canvas's own timebase, and `Siege.Combatant.wasAt` gives the drawing
   somewhere to walk a body **from**. Guarded by `BattleBeatTests`.
3. **Nobody had ever seen the siege screen** (rule 104). Not because raids are
   rare — two centuries measure 127 of them — but because a tick is two real
   minutes and a raid is under one, so nearly every one opened and finished
   inside a catch-up. `TickEngine.advance(_:ticks:registry:stoppingWhen:)` and
   `GameEngine.openSession(…stoppingWhen:)` let the years stop at the first
   raid; what is left is still owed, in `lastRealTimestamp`. **Once per return**
   — `GameViewModel.haltedForRaid`, cleared when the app goes to background —
   because a fortnight away holds several raids and halting at each is a queue.
4. **The workshop avalanche** (rule 105, and §1 of the previous handoff).
   Measured whole-book: 101 recipes waited on nothing but a bench from a later
   age, 91 of them at the medieval `workshop`. `work_shelter` ("Kolna") is the
   bench the first age has; **99 recipes moved to it**. The split needed no
   taste — a recipe stays at the workshop if it eats something smelted or mined,
   or if what it makes is outside its age's damage band, which `ContentTests`
   was already checking.

   |---|---|---|
   | makeable in the first age | 210 | 298 |
   | arriving at medieval | **+119** | +24 |
   | stranded behind a later bench | 101 | 23 |
   | strictly dominated recipes | 19 | 14 |

## Jobs, in the order I would do them

### 1. The iron half of the same avalanche

The 23 recipes still stranded are almost all one shape: `iron_ingot` is
reachable in the **first age** (the bloomery), and everything made of iron waits
for the medieval `workshop`. That is rule 105 one age up. The bands say where
they may go — an iron sword is damage-banded to `ancient`, not to the first age
— so the shape is probably a `smithy` at `ancient`, and the split is again
derivable rather than a matter of taste. **Measure recipes-per-age either side**
(rule 72); the two guards in `ContentTests` will catch an overshoot.

### 2. The fourteen recipes nobody would choose

Measured and now capped by "A second way of making a thing is worth taking".
Every one is the same shape: a generator wrote a second, dearer route to
something the book could already make. **They are deliberately not deleted** —
the panel folds them out of sight and a recipe id can be sitting in a standing
order in a save. The open decision is whether to cut them or to make each the
*good* route at its own age by pricing it below its rival. My recommendation is
the latter, and it wants a rule ("a later route never costs more than an earlier
one") rather than fourteen hand-set numbers.

### 3. The mine — untouched from the last handoff

`iron_ore` falls from 309 at year 90 to 1 at year 200 against seventeen miners.
`WoodProbe` has the columns and nobody has read them against what a mine *can*
yield. Print the distribution first (rules 23, 90).

### 4. `GameViewModel.swift` — still 2400 lines, still for the stated reason

`world` is `private(set)`, which is *file* scope for the setter, and that one
modifier is what keeps a view from writing the simulation. Lift out separate
**types**, not methods: the recipe list is a pure derivation over
`(registry, settlement)`.

### 5. Accessibility, still untouched

Dynamic Type is ignored by 33 fixed `.font(.system(size:))` calls. The canvas
does have a VoiceOver label. `preferredColorScheme(.dark)` is hard-wired and
that one is arguably correct — the game is a night valley — but it should be a
decision written down rather than a default nobody revisited.

## One thing I noticed and did not fix

When a raid ends **while the world is paused for it**, the aftermath on the
canvas clears at once instead of lingering: the linger is measured off
`continuousTick`, which is not moving. The report card fills the gap, so it
reads as abrupt rather than broken. It is rule 103's shape a third time and the
same cure — real seconds, not world ticks — if it turns out to matter.
